## 2. Winning approach: what makes PRAHAR different

Ordered by score impact per hour of work.

### P0: required, highest weight
1. **Trishuli case study vs EMSR927** (30 % + 25 %).
   - Run the pipeline on the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli corridor for 26 Aug 2026. Download the EMSR927 vectors **for checking only**.
   - Report flooded-area IoU, precision, recall, building-hit agreement and cut-off agreement, plus an **error map** (agreements in green, false alarms in red, misses in blue).
   - This is also our **unseen Himalayan evaluation** for the AI component.
2. **Sentinel-2 when clear.** For each before/after date, pick the least-cloudy S2 L2A scene. Compute the **MNDWI water change** and a **debris index** (NDVI loss plus brightness gain on valley floors), using the SCL cloud mask. Fuse: S1 AI water ∪ S2 water where clear; debris = S1 change ∧ S2 debris evidence. That raises the low-confidence debris layer to **medium confidence where optical agrees**.
3. **Live "Run any area" from the dashboard.** Draw or type a bbox and a date, which calls `POST /space/run`. That launches the pipeline in the background with progress steps (searching S1 → downloading → AI → damage → cut-off → sitrep), then the page loads the results. A **fast mode** at 20 m with tile caching gives 3–5 min. This is how judges will test it.

### P1: novelties that set us apart (cheap, high wow)
4. **Debris-aware dual evidence.** Most teams will detect only water. Himalayan disasters (Dharali, Kedarnath, Trishuli) are **debris flows**. We show water (AI) and debris (radar change, optical confirmation) separately, with confidence levels. Story: "our model knew Dharali wasn't a lake."
5. **Mountain-correct radar.** We quantified that the standard UN-SPIDER slope mask **erases 99.6 % of a Himalayan valley** and replaced it with physical layover/shadow geometry. Put this before/after figure in the report and video.
6. **Helicopter landing-zone finder for cut-off villages** (cut-off 20 %).
   - For each cut-off settlement, find nearby **flat, dry, open patches**: Copernicus DEM slope < 5°, ≥ 30 × 30 m, not flooded or debris, no OSM building, within 2 km.
   - List them with coordinates in the sitrep. Rescuers can act on this directly.
7. **People-impact estimate per settlement.** Count pre-event OSM buildings per cut-off settlement → "≈ N buildings isolated". Rank settlements by isolation × size, so the sitrep shows *who first*.
8. **Trilingual sitrep: English, Nepali and Hindi.** Template-based: translated sentence templates, with numbers filled only from the system, so **no LLM and no hallucination risk**. It meets the copilot's language spirit for the Trishuli/Nepal case and boosts usability (10 %).
9. **Flood-path tracer (bonus).** D8 flow routing on GLO-30 from any clicked upstream point, giving the downstream path, the settlements, bridges and roads within the river corridor along it, and the distance from the source. Interactive on the map.
10. **Per-pixel confidence map.** Export the AI flood probability. The dashboard shades by confidence and the sitrep states the share of flooded area above 0.9 probability. "Trustworthy answers" is the brief's own wording.

### P2: polish
11. **Before/after swipe** in the dashboard (S1 radar or S2 true colour), which plays well in the demo video.
12. **Run timings** shown in the sitrep ("answered in 4 min 12 s from raw data"), backing the "fast enough to matter" claim.
13. **Graceful failure:** clear messages when there's no S1 pair, no OSM or full cloud ("S2 unavailable, 100 % cloud: radar only").
14. **Render API fix,** or document a local API only. The Vercel static site is enough for viewing.
